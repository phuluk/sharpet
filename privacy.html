Privacy

Last updated: 30 July 2026. This page is available in English only.

Sharpet is a quiz app. This page describes exactly what it stores, why, and how to get rid of it. If something here is unclear, ask before signing up.

Playing as a guest

Nothing about you is stored. Guest games are held in the browser tab and disappear when you close it. The one thing kept on your device is your language choice, in localStorage under the key sharpet_lang, so the site opens in the same language next time. No analytics, no advertising, no tracking cookies, no third-party scripts — fonts and all code are served from this domain.

With an account

Creating an account stores:

  • Your e-mail address and a hash of your password. These are held by Supabase Auth. The password itself is never stored — only a bcrypt hash — and it is never visible to us or to this application.
  • A display name, which you choose at sign-up. If you leave it blank it defaults to the part of your e-mail before the @.
  • Your quiz history: which domains you played, in which language, when, and whether each answer was right or wrong.
  • Questions you report as incorrect, so an admin can review them.

That is the complete list. No IP logs beyond what the hosting providers keep for operational security, no device fingerprinting, no profiling.

Who can see it

Your history, your reports and your profile are readable only by you. This is enforced in the database with row-level security, not just in the app — the browser is given credentials that cannot reach another account's rows. If you accept a friend request, that person can see your display name; they still cannot see your e-mail address or your answers.

Where it lives

Data is stored in a Supabase Postgres database. The site itself is served by Vercel. Both are processors acting on our behalf; both may keep short-lived request logs for security and abuse prevention.

How long

Your data is kept until you delete your account. Reported questions may be kept after that with the reporter's identity removed, because the report itself is about the question, not about you.

Your rights

If you are in the EU/EEA, the GDPR gives you the right to access, correct, export, restrict, object to, and delete your data. To use any of them, e-mail the address below. Deletion requests are honoured; there is no retention exception we rely on to refuse one. You can also complain to your national data protection authority.

Contact

Write to the maintainer of this deployment. If you are looking at this page on a fork or a self-hosted copy, the operator of that copy is the controller, not the original author.
